It started from my pigeon book.

Installation Heights of Street Furniture and Observed Pigeon Use, New York City

From the book, I wanted two perspectives on the city: the animals’ and the humans’. So I started on the human side.

TRACE · survey

I asked people to draw their memory of a city as one line.

Then: which animals and plants do you remember there?

Someone told me: the happier they were, the more city animals they noticed.

So could the animals we already know bring back our memories of the city? Even the details we never look at, like a streetlight, or anything up high, out of our sight.

What surprised me

Making a work that starts from people’s emotions and memories.

My work usually starts from research and facts.

This time, my approach is everyday life.

As many ways as I could to leave a memory, or help one come back.

That was the happy part: I got to try different ways in. A physical piece couldn’t hold all of them at once.

I also wanted people to leave traces on the website. What they record there is another way to create memory.

Process

How do I bring people to the website?

Looking for a way in from the real world.

What remains unresolved

Why does looking for pigeons matter to the audience?

My answer for now: looking for pigeons helps you build a deeper memory with the city.

But I’m missing the theory behind it.
